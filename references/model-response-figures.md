# Model response curves and surfaces

Use for GAM and other fitted response-shape figures. Visual rationale: G1 in [the two-image record](reference-observations-2026-10-25-gam-kalman.md). These are design and analysis-contract rules, not a fitted model or a claim that the reference calculations are correct. Create actual figures with saved R code executed in RStudio.

## Define what is being plotted

- Identify the estimand before selecting the layer: a conditional mean with other covariates fixed, a centred smooth-term contribution, a marginal average/PDP, an ALE quantity or a prediction for a new observation. Do not label these interchangeably. Record response/link scale, units, offsets and fixed values or averaging population/weights as relevant.
- Record model family/link, relevant terms, fitting/smoothing method, data provenance and analysis sample unit. Do not infer an interaction, cyclic basis, significance or causal effect from a curve's shape. Training fit alone does not establish held-out predictive accuracy.
- Distinguish simulated reference, fitted estimate and observed measurements using labels and line/mark redundancy. Real data without a known response function must not acquire a fabricated truth curve.

## Curves, support and uncertainty

- Use one varying input per conditional curve, explain what is held fixed, and show training support via rug, density or another unobtrusive device. A rug encodes observed input locations, not response variability, independent sample size or uncertainty width.
- State whether a band represents mean estimation, new-observation prediction or another quantity; name its level, construction, assumptions and pointwise versus simultaneous scope. A pointwise mean band does not cover an entire function or future observations by default. When transforming scales, compute and transform intervals consistently with the estimand, not just the central line.
- Check sparse regions and extrapolation; do not extend a smooth curve as if supported by data without a visible warning/mask. Do not narrow a band or hide an unfavourable region for aesthetic balance.
- For a periodic input, declare the period, endpoint convention and whether the fitted model actually imposes cyclic boundary conditions. Align curve and surface domains accordingly; do not stitch endpoints or duplicate boundary observations merely to make the graphic look periodic.
- Comparable response axes help compare magnitudes; different explicitly labelled ranges can help compare shapes. If claiming relative effect size, use common scales or defined contrasts/standardization, not the apparent height of independently scaled curves.

## Joint prediction surfaces

- Name both varying inputs, their units, fixed remaining covariates and the response scale. A curved prediction surface or contour can arise without an interaction term; infer interaction only from the model specification and an appropriate analysis, not visual curvature.
- Check joint training support, not just separate axis ranges/rugs; mask or annotate unsupported input combinations. Identify grid resolution, interpolation and missing/out-of-range behaviour. Do not manufacture visual detail beyond model/data support.
- Use an ordered palette for magnitude and a diverging palette only for a meaningful signed centre. Keep response units, limits and contour levels explicit; thin contours should clarify readings, not imply uncertainty or significance. Retain a separate colourbar from model-identity legends.

For factorial or background-dependent responses, consider an aligned factor-state strip beside the measurements, fitted agreement, and selected response slices only when each adds distinct evidence. Join the strip and measurements by validated combination IDs and use one ordering. Define the additive, multiplicative or other reference on its analysis scale before comparing fitted responses; compute contrasts before display transformations. A log axis or curved surface does not identify that reference for the reader. A 3-D surface is optional: prefer a flat contour view and labelled slices when perspective hides comparisons. Keep any surface, slices and reference derived from the same model, units and supported domain. See [the inspected response-surface case](reference-observations-2026-11-04-response-surfaces-marginals.md) for rationale and source limitations.

## Conditional relationships and ensemble marginals

- For continuous driver × outcome × scenario records, use the joint scatter for the conditional relationship; add an x-distribution or outcome summary only to answer a separate marginal question. Align the corresponding coordinates. Conditional quantiles, marginal percentiles and mean-confidence bands must retain different names and calculations.
- Preserve model, draw and scenario IDs and their weights. An input density based on distinct model realizations may differ from one obtained by pooling every repeated simulation draw; define the intended population before reducing the table. Point darkness and simulation count do not by themselves represent probability or empirical independent n.
- Use named scenario colours consistently in the linked panels, neutral interval fills and a clearly identified fitted line. Keep other colour domains, such as crop identity, in separate mappings rather than silently collecting their legends. Inspect marks after compositing, at final size and in grayscale/colour-vision views.
- An equivalent secondary outcome axis is permissible only through one explicit, invertible conversion with a fixed denominator and documented population/year; verify paired ticks. Do not use it for an unrelated outcome or a row-dependent denominator. Ordered decile boxes need a discrete/grouped axis, not fictitious equal numeric spacing.

Read [the ensemble/marginal case](reference-observations-2026-11-04-response-surfaces-marginals.md) when applying these distinctions; its boxplots, preprocessing and interval definitions are source-specific, not defaults to copy.

## Figure and caption checks

Combine complementary views only when they answer different parts of the question. Collect legends only for identical encodings. Check overlap of fit/reference lines, interval visibility, rug density, endpoint behaviour, surface support, contour labels and final-size text. Captions must distinguish conditioning from averaging and mean uncertainty from predictive uncertainty. Report the estimated response shape without converting association or model dependence into causation. Do not claim these checks passed until actually run on the new figure/data.

## Mixture responses on a simplex

For three nonnegative fractions on a common basis that sum to one within a stated tolerance, a ternary plot can compare mixture compositions. Fix corner order, tick orientation and equal aspect across panels; mark measured mixtures separately from model-evaluated grid points. Require a supplied fit, named response and valid support before drawing a model surface; require stated constraints and units before showing their intersection as a feasible window. Label finite-grid selection as a grid optimum, not a global optimum. Separate response, secondary objectives and mean-response SE/prediction uncertainty with their own keys; a feasible mean response does not itself demonstrate robust feasibility under uncertainty. Store closure tolerance, grid, limits, masks and constraints in the project configuration, and test samples/contours against data before final-size review. These are design instructions, not a bundled ternary renderer. See [reference review](reference-observations-2026-11-09-network-robust-mixture.md).

## Spectra and predictive evidence

For keyed samples measured across ordered wavelengths, an overlaid spectrum plus a linked wavelength inset can show input support; use outcome colouring only when that outcome is available for the shown samples and label units/transform. Pair with coefficient, training-only tuning and held-out observed–predicted views only when the corresponding model outputs, split and methods exist. Distinguish coefficient pointwise bootstrap intervals at fixed selected dimension from intervals that repeat model selection, simultaneous bands and new-observation prediction intervals; document resampling unit, preprocessing, fit population and selected model. Verify independent evaluation IDs and that preprocessing/tuning respect each training fold; report comparison baseline and do not infer causal wavelengths from correlated coefficients. Configure crop, limits, unit, colour mapping, selection marker and band semantics in the project; audit sample identities, curves and final-size legibility. The scaffold does not supply a spectral modelling renderer. See [reference evidence](reference-observations-2026-11-10-spectral-multiscale.md).

## Frozen-input stress and refit instability

Use when keyed held-out observations, frozen fitted models and a defined input-corruption grid are available. Pair a signed metric-difference field with a labelled slice of absolute errors; add case-level refit intervals only when a separate resampling experiment supplies them. This separates relative advantage, absolute performance and training instability. A heatmap of differences alone can conceal two poor models. Design evidence: [sensor-stress reference](reference-observations-2026-11-12-robustness-sensitivity-ranking.md).

- Compute both models' errors on the same cases and units for every grid cell; declare subtraction direction, centre the diverging scale at zero and label which sign favours which model. The zero contour is equal error, not a significance boundary. Retain cell geometry and failed/missing cells; a tree model's irregular response does not justify cosmetic smoothing.
- Mark the unchanged-input origin and the exact slice used in the absolute-error panel. Check its metric differences against the corresponding field cells. Keep truth labels fixed only when the experiment is explicitly measurement corruption, not a physical intervention or future-climate forecast. State what is frozen, altered or retrained and where the perturbed inputs leave training support.
- Preserve test IDs through bootstrap predictions; state resampling unit, training-only fitting/tuning, dependence assumptions, model complexity and seeds. Refit quantiles reflect that specified refitting process, not residual noise or an unseen observation's predictive interval. Match baseline/refit algorithms and tuning budgets when claiming isolated resampling variability; otherwise disclose the difference as another source of variation.
- A case-level display may order rows by observed response to expose tail errors, provided it says so and retains every eligible case. That ordering is not chronology and cannot support a temporal-trend claim. Pair each observed mark with its predicted summary and named range; disclose exclusions and do not infer simultaneous coverage from marginal ranges.
- In the copied project's analysis/configuration, add and wire `bias_grid`, `frozen_model_id`, `slice_value`, `difference_order`, `case_order`, `resample_unit` and `interval_kind`; draw from checked tables with `geom_tile`/`geom_rect`, labelled contours, lines and `geom_linerange`. Reconcile the origin, slice and case joins; inspect range/observation overlaps and pale comparison lines. None of this creates a new fitted-model implementation in the scaffold.
