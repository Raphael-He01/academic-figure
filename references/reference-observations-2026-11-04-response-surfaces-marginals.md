## S1 — connect factorial evidence, model agreement and a response surface

### Purpose and panel roles

| Panels | Visible encoding | Why this choice can help; inference rather than author intent |
|---|---|---|
| a–b | Small regulatory diagram and a vertical crossing/measurement workflow | Establish what the factors mean before many combinations are compared. The two arrow systems need separate semantics: regulation versus workflow. |
| c | Wide distribution/summary display over a four-row genotype tile matrix, with counts between them | One column key connects response, sample support and factor state without requiring a long genotype name at every point. A mean-only bar chart would hide repeated values and their frequency. |
| d–e | Two rows of three comparable scatter panels; horizontal and vertical intervals, identity guides, dashed fits, selected leader labels | Paired background comparisons expose changing slopes and uncertainty in both coordinates. A single pooled trend would mix scientifically different backgrounds. |
| f | Predicted-versus-estimated response scatter and equality line | Checks agreement of model output with estimated responses; this panel alone does not establish external validation. |
| g–h | Light 3-D wireframe, emphasized model paths and reference marks; four small mutation-distance profiles | Surface explains joint behaviour; profiles make selected departures from a baseline readable without relying entirely on perspective. Neither view alone proves interaction. |

### Detailed design card

1. **Layout — Visible:** a/b form a narrow explanatory column; c occupies the broad upper data area; repeated d/e views align horizontally; f/g/h close with agreement, surface and interpretable profiles. **Proposal:** allocate area to label density and evidence roles rather than giving a small process diagram the same width as a many-column matrix. The source's full eight-letter arrangement is not a template.
2. **Typography/strokes — Visible:** bold panel letters are stronger than axes and annotations; genotype labels use scientific notation and italics; small black points and fine interval strokes coexist with a stronger dashed fitted line and a pale identity diagonal. Surface mesh is lighter than emphasized trajectories. **Unknown:** original family, point sizes, stroke widths and exact opacity.
3. **Colour — Visible:** almost entirely black, grey and white; small plant illustrations add muted green/yellow but do not encode the quantitative outcomes. Matrix states use white/grey/black; baseline marks and labels in g/h are grey. **Mechanism:** reducing hue competition directs attention to geometry and comparison. **Adapt:** separate state, estimate and baseline keys; identical grey does not make them the same variable.
4. **Annotations — Visible:** counts sit immediately above their genotype columns; selected points receive leaders; h labels the cases directly; g distinguishes mesh, paths and reference points. **Proposal:** choose annotations by a stated scientific role and stable ID, not merely an extreme value. Dense gene labels and pale reference text need final-size collision and contrast checks.
5. **Axes/statistics — Caption/context:** c represents one population-season subset, uses mean ± SD and circle area for counts; d–f intervals are 95% CIs, with extremely imprecise combinations omitted under the stated rule. g/h compare against a multiplicative baseline. **Proposal:** preserve the estimand, sample hierarchy and exclusion record; do not apply this paper's exclusion threshold to a new dataset. Total least-squares fits in d/e are not interchangeable with an automatic ordinary least-squares line.
6. **Limits — Visible/context:** artwork b states 27,000 inflorescences while the article text reports 35,606 overall. Whether this reflects a subset or another distinction was not established. Record the discrepancy without selecting a convenient count. n in c is an observation count, not automatically independent plant replication. No model coefficients or CIs were recomputed.
7. **Export/scale — Visible:** the image is tall and dense; the web asset permits enlarged inspection, but small n labels, gene annotations and numerous ticks are vulnerable to reduction. **Unknown:** physical placement size, vector content, embedded fonts and production DPI. Colour-vision simulation and print-size verification were not run.
8. **Transfer — adopt:** keyed metadata strip, explicit baseline and matched background comparisons. **Adapt:** choose a flat contour map with labelled slices when quantitative readings matter; allow a surface only when its explanatory advantage survives occlusion checks. **Reject:** colourizing every genotype, copying all panels, treating fitted agreement as held-out evidence, or concealing wide intervals to simplify the view.

## S2 — combine a response relationship with two different marginal questions

### Purpose and panel roles

| Panels | Visible encoding | Why this choice can help; inference rather than author intent |
|---|---|---|
| a | Crop-coloured trajectories followed by grouped end-of-century boxplots, with brackets and one shaded condition | Separates temporal evolution from the spread of endpoint projections. The boxplots are grouped summaries, not extra years on the time axis. |
| b–c | Stacked compact panels of blue boxes and whiskers by temperature/income decile | Exposes stratified variation; these are not evidence-free mean bars. Equal visual spacing represents ordered groups, not equal numeric temperature/income increments. |
| d main | Dense blue/red-orange point ensembles, black response curve and two neutral bands | Retains scenario identity while the black curve anchors the shared response trend. Thin small marks reveal vertical spread without assigning a hue to every simulation. |
| d lower/right | Aligned warming densities below; two scenario summary boxes on the right, with percentile callouts | Bottom asks where scenarios lie along the driver; right asks about their overall outcomes. Neither marginal is the conditional interval at a fixed driver. |

### Detailed design card

1. **Layout — Visible:** broad temporal panel, narrow two-panel stratification column, then the largest joint-response panel. d's lower panel shares x positions; its separate right summary shares outcome meaning. **Proposal:** keep these coordinate links mechanically aligned and allow independent density height; use a small callout only when it prevents a likely misreading.
2. **Typography/strokes — Visible:** panel tags stand out, axes and legends use compact plain text, the central curve is stronger than point marks, reference zero is quiet. Percentile callouts have curved leaders. b/c numeric group labels rotate vertically. **Adapt:** shorten or reflow labels before rotating; test the lower-density strip and smallest labels at manuscript size. No exact font/pt inference.
3. **Colour — Visible:** six crop colours in a; a single blue for b/c; blue and red-orange for scenarios in d; grey bands and a black fit. **Mechanism:** variation in hue is spent on identity, while neutral lightness separates intervals. **Adapt:** give crops and scenarios separate named colour maps and legends; similar hues across these panels do not imply the same entity. Add redundant labels/line types for scenario marginals; grayscale and colour-vision performance remain untested.
4. **Annotations — Visible:** brackets distinguish endpoint conditions, shading identifies one condition, and right-hand arrows explain percentile positions. Some upper whiskers in a extend with arrows beyond the visible range. **Proposal:** explain any overflow indicator and retain full values in the analysis; it is not permission to trim tails. This figure needs no invented significance stars.
5. **Axes/statistics — Caption:** d points are climate-model × Monte Carlo outcomes; bands are conditional 10–90% and 25–75% quantiles. The bottom uses 33 climate models/surrogates; right boxes collapse outcomes by scenario. Its second y-axis rescales against 2015 production of the six studied crops. a–c use disclosed winsorization before percentage conversion. **Proposal:** different grains/weights need explicit contracts; do not count simulation draws as independent empirical samples, call quantile bands CIs, or recreate the lower density by blindly pooling point rows.
6. **Limits — Unknown:** raw weights, density bandwidth, quantile calculation details and all simulation values were not audited. Overplotted darker columns can reflect repeated driver values; darkness alone is not scenario likelihood. Negative tails and the chosen fitting function need analytical review, not aesthetic removal.
7. **Export/scale — Visible:** a wide composite suits a landscape allocation, but the single web raster cannot prove a journal placement width. **Unknown:** source vector master, embedding, print resolution or final-size compliance. Actual source dimensions are in the record above; no numeric style settings were inferred from them.
8. **Transfer — adopt:** distinguish conditional spread from input and outcome marginals; keep neutral uncertainty behind identifiable points and a clear fit. **Adapt:** use fewer views when only one question is needed; show an explicitly categorical decile axis. **Reject:** averaging away scenario variation to obtain a tidy line, treating density height as a probability without normalization, or adding a second unrelated outcome to the secondary axis.

## Operational transfer and hypothetical application

| Data eligibility / design mechanism | Existing R implementation location or proposed change | Check when actually implemented |
|---|---|---|
| Factor combinations plus measurements, sample IDs and a fitted model | Project data preparation joins by combination/season ID; use one shared factor order for ggplot response and tile layers; existing `cfg$group_levels` supplies ordering where applicable | One-to-one keys, identical column order, count definition, intervals and zero handling; no independent resorting of the strip |
| Valid two-input predictions and a specified additive/multiplicative reference | New project prediction table for heatmap/contours and selected profiles; put support mask, reference definition, slices and transformation in saved configuration | Same grid/model/units for the surface and slices, quantified reference contrasts, supported regions and visible null definition |
| Continuous driver, scenario and nested ensemble identifiers | Project main scatter plus patchwork marginals; configure scenario colours, named quantile levels, density bandwidth, weights and draw grouping separately | Marginal coordinate alignment; intervals not swapped for CIs; weights/grain reconciled; no pooling that overweights repeated model draws |
| Fixed denominator for an equivalent secondary scale | Project `sec_axis()` transform and inverse tied to a documented denominator; skip if denominator varies between points | Several corresponding tick pairs satisfy the same conversion; denominator population/year appears in caption |

Existing `R/figure_config.R` in the bundled R template supplies physical size, category colours/shapes/line types, line/point size and panel spacing. New fields above are **proposed project parameters**, not implemented scaffold features; some bundled band/layout settings remain local to the example. No asset or plotting code was changed.

Hypothetical task: given scenario × climate-model × simulation-draw records with warming and yield impact, first verify row grain, weights and baseline; then choose a scatter plus a justified conditional summary. Add an input density only from its properly defined model-level distribution and an outcome boxplot only if the marginal comparison is requested. A mean-only table supports a simpler estimate plot, not reconstructed distributions. Real production would execute saved R code in RStudio, inspect final-size exports and run applicable QA; none of that occurred in this reference-only batch.
