# Journal requirements: provisional profile and verification

Requirements are temporally unstable and often differ by article type and submission stage. Recheck the target's official author/artwork pages for every real submission and record access date, relevant section, final width, maximum height, fonts, minimum line weight, color space, format, raster resolution by artwork type, and whether separate panels or one composite are requested.

## Generic high-level profile when no target is supplied

- Build at intended final width: approximately 89 mm for a single-column figure or 180–183 mm for a double-column figure.
- Use a plain sans-serif font with a restrained hierarchy; start near 7–8 pt for ordinary labels at final size and never assume a smaller value is acceptable.
- Keep thin rules visible after reduction; begin near 0.4–0.7 mm for primary plotted lines and inspect the exported result.
- Prefer PDF/SVG for line art and plots; provide TIFF/PNG only when requested and choose resolution from the target's artwork class.
- Keep panel tags, axes, units, legend grammar and semantic encodings consistent.
- Submit RGB or CMYK only as the target requests. Confirm color accessibility and grayscale meaning.

These values are starting points, not a claim of compliance with a specific journal.

## Nature main journal: page-specific verification (2026-10-17)

- The [general formatting guide](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide), directly read on this date, gives 90/180 mm widths and a 170 mm page depth. It requires logically connected panels, concise captions, defined error bars/statistics and scale bars rather than magnification factors.
- The [final-submission guide](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/final-submission), also directly read, applies when an editor requests production files. It gives 89/183 mm widths, optional 120–136 mm intermediate width and 247 mm full page depth; these numbers differ from the general page. Use the applicable submission-stage instructions and editor specifications, recording the page and date; do not silently merge these sizes into one official preset.
- That final page specifies Arial/Helvetica-like sans-serif lettering, 5–7 pt ordinary text, 8 pt bold upright panel letters, and 0.25–1 pt strokes at final size (approximately 0.088–0.353 mm). The generic scaffold's thicker data strokes and larger labels are not a Nature final-artwork preset: override them for that destination and inspect legibility.
- Preserve editable vector text/lines; the page accepts vector graph formats including PDF. Photographic raster material is at least 300 dpi at maximum intended use size, with 300–600 dpi guidance; RGB is recommended. These raster instructions do not replace vector export for plots and do not automatically apply to Extended Data.
- The separately linked `research-figure-guide.nature.com` site failed to load during this review. Do not mark that page as newly verified or claim these two pages settle every current production detail.

## Earlier official examples (accessed 2026-10-09, not newly verified)

- Science's initial-manuscript instructions list common widths of 5.7, 12.1 and 18.4 cm and recommend roughly 7 pt type, not below 5 pt, with Helvetica and sufficiently visible lines. Revised-manuscript rules must be checked again because they may differ.
- Elsevier's official artwork overview, FAQ and sizing pages provide a broad publisher fallback where a Cell Press page is inaccessible. Treat publisher-wide advice as secondary to the target journal's own page.

Science's author-guideline request encountered an access challenge on 2026-10-17; the earlier values above are historical notes, not a fresh compliance check. Learning a published Science figure's design does not establish its current submission requirements.
