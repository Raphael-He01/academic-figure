# Organizing evidence across panels

Read when designing, restructuring or reviewing labelled multi-panel figures or their order in a manuscript. These are reasoning aids, not official journal panel-count requirements.

## A question shared by the figure

Use the [figure contract](figure-contract.md) to identify the central question first. Several subordinate findings may belong together when they establish or qualify that question. A separate major inference usually needs another figure.

For each panel, record its question, evidence role, comparator, unique inference, dependency on other panels and intended destination. Panel letters indicate reading order; they do not prescribe which chart must come first.

## Select necessary roles

| Role | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| Setup | Defines the system, intervention or comparison |
| Concrete example | Makes a phenomenon observable while stating selection limits |
| Main estimate | Establishes the magnitude or performance central to the question |
| Comparator or control | Tests a credible alternative explanation |
| Decomposition or strata | Locates contributions or meaningful variation |
| Independent validation | Checks the inference through another measurement or evidence source |
| Perturbation | Tests a specified failure prediction or sensitivity |
| Boundary | Shows where a conclusion weakens or stops applying |
| Mechanistic support | Constrains an explanation without exceeding the study design |

Not every figure needs every role. Another metric is not automatically another inference. Before adding a panel, state what would become unsupported if it were removed.

## Four ways to connect evidence

1. **Credibility under challenges:** define the capability, measure it, test distinct ways it could fail, then state the surviving scope. Group diagnostics by the claim they challenge.
2. **Aggregate to example:** establish a population pattern, quantify or localize it, and use a justified example or exception to explain its meaning. The example must contribute more than decoration.
3. **Question refinement:** a test changes the hypothesis and motivates the next test. Retain inferential turning points, not a chronological inventory of work.
4. **Increasing demands on a method:** explain behavior, compare alternatives, test harder conditions, then examine broader applicability. Do not repeat the same ranking in successive figures.

These evidence chains are separate from the four spatial compositions in the contract. Choose the chain from the research question and the composition from the required marks, labels and comparisons.

## Space and order

- Give decisive evidence a clear entry point and enough area to judge it. A deliberately uniform grid is appropriate for repeated comparisons; an oversized lead panel is not mandatory.
- Keep controls legible even when visually quieter. Failure cases that qualify the claim must remain discoverable.
- Reuse identities, units and comparable scales. Disclose changed cohorts, masks, denominators, estimators or uncertainty instead of relying on similar appearance.
- Let a later figure answer a question raised by the preceding result. If two figures support the same narrow statement, combine them or make their different inferential jobs explicit.
- Map panel order to the caption and Results argument without requiring the caption to repeat the full interpretation.

## Main figure or supporting material

Keep decisive comparisons, necessary controls and conclusion-changing exceptions in the main argument. Secondary views, expanded provenance and robustness that does not alter interpretation may move to supporting material. A distinct claim belongs in another figure. Remove duplicated evidence only after checking that it adds no independent information.

At planning and final review, ask:

- Does each panel add a needed inference rather than only visual variety?
- Are the primary comparison and its limitations visible together?
- Can readers follow dependencies without repeatedly searching unrelated legends?
- Do the figure sequence and written claims agree about what was established?
- Are panel roles still readable at the placed publication size?
