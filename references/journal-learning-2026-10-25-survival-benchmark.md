## S1 — Spatial immune evidence linked to survival

**Identity and access.** Sorin, Rezanejad, Karimi et al., *Single-cell spatial landscapes of the lung tumour immune microenvironment*, **Nature** 614, 548–554 (2023), DOI `10.1038/s41586-022-05672-3`. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05672-3) · [Figure 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05672-3/figures/3). Published/version of record 2023-02-01; issue date 2023-02-16. This is Nature main journal. The public publisher figure page and caption were inspected in the browser; the primary agent also viewed its 2112 × 950 PNG pixels and checked relevant article methods. No patient data or supplementary analyses were reproduced.

**Discovery status.** dsh suggested the figure after two bounded searches, but reported that both attempted page reads were blocked. Its returned title was the figure title, not the article title. Its initial record was therefore a search lead, not visual evidence. The primary agent corrected the identity and completed the review independently.

| Dimension | What the inspected source supports | Transfer and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Question and chart roles | Cell-population/marker embeddings, a neighbourhood-enrichment matrix and high/low overall-survival comparisons connect cellular characterization to patient-level association. Caption defines 416 patients, multiple cell counts, a zero threshold on standardized prevalence and log-rank comparisons. | Connect the representation, measurement and outcome evidence; preserve the change from cell-level measurements to patient-level inference. These are observational groups, not randomized treatments. |
| Layout and reading order | A wide composite places three aligned embedding/curve columns at left/centre, a tall neighbourhood heatmap at right and two further survival plots below the left columns. Shared white space separates each role. | Allocate width by information density. This particular asymmetric layout is not a default; a narrow journal layout may require a split figure. The two bottom survival comparisons need local identifying titles in a new figure rather than only left/right explanation in the caption. |

The neighbourhood heatmap uses a separate blue–white–red scale. | Preserve semantic separation, not the exact colours. Prefer a perceptually ordered, tested sequential palette for marker magnitude. Do not treat the marker scale and neighbourhood scale as interchangeable or claim the source passed/failed colour-vision tests. |
| Axes and uncertainty | Survival uses years and 0–100% with step curves, small tick-like marks, baseline n and local P values. No risk table or confidence band is visible. **Adopt:** linked evidence panels, consistent group identity, common survival scales, compact local statistical annotations. **Adapt:** supply local labels for every survival comparison, risk-table support and suitable uncertainty; use an ordered marker scale. **Do not transfer:** an unexplained cutoff, P-value-only interpretation, rainbow-like magnitude palette as a default, exact panel composition or source-derived biological conclusions.

**R mapping:** existing `group_levels`, `group_colors`, `group_linetypes`, font/stroke/spacing fields apply. Survival endpoint, event coding, time origin, interval construction and risk-table extraction must be added to the project analysis/configuration. The current scaffold does not implement them. [Survival rules](survival-figures.md) incorporate the independently checked R method documentation and counting conventions.

## B1 — Metric matrix linked to diagnostic embeddings

**Identity and access.** Luecken et al., *Benchmarking atlas-level data integration in single-cell genomics*, **Nature Methods** 19, 41–50 (2022), DOI `10.1038/s41592-021-01336-8`, published online 2021-12-23. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01336-8) · [Figure 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01336-8/figures/2). This is a Nature family journal, **not Nature main journal**. It resolves candidate P1 in [the earlier record](journal-learning-2026-10-25.md).

**Evidence.** The primary agent viewed the full 1796 × 2076 publisher PNG, read the figure caption and the public article's “Ranking and metric aggregation” and visualization methods. A secondary reviewer also reported image inspection but reached its usage limit before completing its method report; the accepted conclusions below come from the primary agent's completed review. No benchmark outputs or plotting source code were recomputed.

| Dimension | What the inspected source supports | Transfer and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | A task-specific ranking summarizes batch correction and biological conservation, then UMAP views show cell identity and batch for the unintegrated baseline and four selected methods. | Numerical summary and diagnostic view answer different questions. Neither attractive mixing nor a high weighted score alone establishes a universal method advantage. |
| Layout | A full-width score matrix sits above two aligned rows of five embeddings. A vertical separator distinguishes the baseline. The matrix explicitly states that 55 middle rows are not displayed. | Align method columns and paired diagnostic rows; mark omitted rows and retain full results. The excerpt is not an exhaustive adjacent-rank comparison. A matrix this dense may need a landscape or supplemental layout. |
| Headers and typography | Rounded group headers distinguish overall, batch and biological categories. Method rows include output icons, feature selection and scaling indicators. Metric names are rotated; category legends sit beneath the embeddings. | Carry configuration identifiers and group metrics by purpose. Rounded pills, large black header and exact row striping are optional author choices. Define abbreviations and inspect rotated labels rather than preserving compactness at any cost. |
| Encodings | Separate ranking-colour and score-size keys are visible. Metric circles vary in size and colour; aggregate scores occupy rectangular bar cells. Embeddings have their own multicolour categorical keys. | Explain rank versus score and their directions separately; verify circle area/score mapping in new code. The source's exact radius/area mapping, per-column ranking ties and colour interpolation were not reconstructed. No exact HEX/alpha or accessibility verdict is inferred. |
| Scientific methods | Article methods min–max scale metrics within each task, average within categories, then combine biological conservation and batch removal with 0.6/0.4 weights. Metrics available depend on output type. Caption links the full task plot elsewhere. | Record normalization bounds, metric coverage, weighting and task scope. The weights are this study's choices. Available-case averages need an explicit coverage policy; they must not silently turn missing metrics into zero. |
| Diagnostics and annotation | Within each method the two rows repeat the same-looking point geometry with different categorical colourings. Cell identity and batch have separate keys. Method names carry output/preprocessing labels, and asterisks occur in some names. | Future code must verify keyed coordinates, not merely visual similarity. The article explains label-assisted methods; asterisks here are not significance stars. Reusing a hue in unrelated category keys does not establish identity correspondence. |
| Axes and export | UMAP panels omit numeric ticks; score legends provide magnitude/rank interpretation. The tall web bitmap makes small metric labels dense. | Name embedding methods even without physical axes. Exact figure dimensions, effective print DPI, vector/editability and font embedding remain unverified. No benchmark replication, final-size rendering, CVD simulation or grayscale test was performed. |

**Method distinctions retained:** Fig. 2 is one task's run-level overview. The article separately describes ranking each method's best preprocessing variant and averaging ranks across tasks for other summaries, including assigning failed tasks the unintegrated rank. Do not automatically apply that cross-task failure or selection rule to Fig. 2 or a new benchmark. The article also reports a normalization sensitivity comparison; this review did not reproduce it. Source colour-rank implementation and all missing-cell cases remain unverified without code/data inspection.

**Adopt:** grouped metrics, explicit configuration identity, honest omission labels, score/rank legends and paired diagnostic views. **Adapt:** more readable metric labels, explicit NA/failure states, weight sensitivity and raw-score access. **Do not transfer:** fixed 40/60 weights, an unexplained percent-as-accuracy interpretation, ranking claims from UMAP geometry or silent best-variant selection.

**R mapping:** maintain a long results table and separate R metric-transformation/aggregation script; use explicit factor order, independent category/rank mappings and area-aware point scaling. Existing style/export fields are reusable; weights, bounds, availability, ties and shown rows require new wired project fields. See [benchmark rules](benchmark-figures.md) and [embedding rules](embedding-atlas-figures.md).

## Batch-level review and hypothetical application

The transferable addition is to show the **support and choices behind a visual summary**: follow-up risk sets behind survival curves, and metric coverage/normalization/weights behind a leaderboard. Both supplement existing rules about denominators and uncertainty, rather than creating a new universal visual style. No new Science figure was verified in this batch; [the preceding Science review](reference-observations-2026-10-25-science-agriculture.md) remains a separate completed record.

**Hypothetical application, not an executed figure:** given a real follow-up table, the skill would first establish endpoint, time origin, event/censor codes, entry times and patient IDs. It would choose survival versus cumulative incidence from the question, build a curve and synchronized risk table from the same analysis, then execute and inspect the saved R code in RStudio. Given real model-evaluation results, it would preserve raw metrics, expose missing/failure states and weights, and pair an aggregate comparison with diagnostics only where needed. Missing scientific definitions would be marked for confirmation; no counts, P values, rankings or findings would be invented.
