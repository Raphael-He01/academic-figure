3. **Type, strokes and marks.** The left map uses a dark dashed regime boundary, a light solid operating contour, a high-contrast outlined reference dot and in-region text. b separates circles and diamonds with small horizontal intervals and thin neutral guides. c layers a dark line over two light bands, with a dotted control reference. On the pale upper-left field the white contour is weak; a labelled dark halo or contrasting stroke is a transfer improvement, not proof the source failed at its original size.
4. **Colour logic.** Cream-to-teal orders stock fraction; it is a magnitude scale, not signed divergence. Blue and rust distinguish index types, while nested teal opacity distinguishes scenario ranges. The pale zero-stock region is meaningful output and must remain distinct from uncomputed cells. c's overlapping bands must preserve their nesting after alpha compositing; the lighter band does not mean missing data. Exact palette values are unknown and the user's default colour card remains unchanged.
5. **Annotations and legends.** Region labels name persistence versus extinction; subtitles distinguish the model boundary from a 20%-stock threshold. b names first versus total order, and c explicitly calls its shaded layers scenario ranges. The central curve's mean/median/reference meaning is not stated clearly enough to adopt. Label the 20% threshold as a chosen criterion unless external evidence supplies its status; it is not automatically an empirical ecological safety limit.
6. **Axes and integrity.** Growth and harvest have inverse-year units; the field is stock/K, b is an output-variance share, and c is biomass per year. These estimands are different, so an input absent from a normalized field can still matter to yield variance. Text states independent uniform input ranges, 20,000 paired base rows, 300 paired bootstraps and 5,000 paired scenario draws; all are unverified. Monte Carlo intervals describe estimator precision, not biological confidence. Total-order indices must not be normalized into a composition or subtracted as isolated pairwise effects. Scenario ranges require distribution and central-statistic definitions. The screenshot supplies no basis to silently infer or retrofit a Sobol design from arbitrary data.
7. **File and scaling review.** Named boundaries and paired sensitivity marks are visible in the supplied raster; small intervals and footer are vulnerable to reduction. The curve's zero-output tail is retained, but numerical consistency of surface, boundary and ribbon has not been computed. Inspect semantic and visual contrast at final size in future work; no fit/simulation, print export or accessibility checks were performed here.
8. **Transfer decision.** Adopt explicit threshold grammar, distinct estimands and nested scenario ranges with stated conditioning. Adapt boundary contrast and state the central statistic. Reject equating scenario spread, Monte Carlo precision and observational uncertainty; also reject smoothing away the zero-output regime. Configure equations/thresholds/draw IDs and range semantics in project analysis, then check boundary consistency and interval order under [boundary and sensitivity rules](engineering-model-figures.md#boundary-maps-sensitivity-indices-and-scenario-envelopes).
